# Chapter 1: The Iron Skiff

The copper hull of the *Cloud-Wisp* shuddered as the four-inch hemp mooring hawser snapped taut against the iron cleat. 

Lyra tightened her grip on the frozen rail, the winter gale biting through the cracked stitching of her calfskin mitts. Three thousand feet below the wooden staging of Dock Forty, the cloud-sea churned like curdled tallow, swallowed by the jagged basalt needles of the Lower Abyss. Rain lashed the leaded glass of the wheelhouse, carrying the stinging stink of soot and hot machine oil from the starboard boiler exhaust.

"Hold the slack, girl!" Captain Orlov barked through his copper breathing cone. His beard was frosted white with frazil ice, each bristle stiff as broom-wire. He kicked a balk of seasoned pine under the rudder stock to jam the counter-weight. "If that winch dogs off before the cargo is slung, we drop twenty fathoms into the soup without a pilot."

Lyra did not answer with words; speech was wasted steam in a force-six squall. She hauled on the wet hemp, her boots slipping on the grease-caked decking until her heel found purchase against an iron rivet. The brass links of the boiler gauge rattled, needle trembling at ninety pounds of steam. She tasted the bitter grit of pulverized coal upon her tongue, washed down with a cold swig of chicory dregs from the tin cup hooked to her utility harness.

Through the salt-streaked viewport, the High Spire rose into the darkening sky, its buttresses hung with row upon row of amber gas lamps. Somewhere within those lead-lined chambers, the Syndicate clerks were tallying the price of cloud-amber in gold coin. Down here on the catwalk, life was measured in inches of hemp and the heat of a single coal fire.

The davit swung out with a shrieking protest of dry bearings. Suspended from the crane hook, the lead-wrapped canister of volatile aether swung into the skiff’s cargo well, settling with a dull, heavy clatter that resonated straight up through the soles of Lyra's boots.

"Lashed!" she shouted into the wind, driving a steel cotter pin through the hasp with three clean blows of her copper-faced mallet. 

Orlov gave a single, satisfied grunt, wiped sleet from his scarred cheek, and threw the main steam valve wide. The boiler coughed, spat a gout of violet flame through the pop-valve, and the little iron skiff leaned into the abyss.
