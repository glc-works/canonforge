# Chapter 2: The Cloud Furnace

The heat hit Captain Orlov like an open hand to the throat the instant he kicked open the firebox hatch.

A roar of red flame flooded the narrow hold, illuminating the sweating rivets of the pressure drum and Lyra’s soot-streaked face. In the freezing gale outside, a man's fingers went white and dead in ten minutes; in here, three feet from the coke-grate, wool scorched if you leaned too close to the flues.

"Three shovels of anthracite," Orlov commanded, wiping stinging grease from his brow with the greasy hem of his sleeve. "Feed the lower flue first. The updraft over the Boiling Shelf will suck the fire right out of the chimney if we lose draft pressure."

Lyra swung the heavy scoop with rhythmic, practiced economy. The black coal clattered across the glowing bed, sizzling with sharp pops of burning sulfur that made her cough into her woolen collar. The four-cylinder reciprocating engine thumped beneath the iron deck-plates—*chug-clank, chug-clank*—a mechanical pulse driving the twin paddle-screws through the boiling fog banks.

Orlov reached for the iron ration tin wedged behind the steam manifold. The tallow-wrapped salt-pork had softened in the engine heat, dripping savory grease across a hunk of crusty sourdough bread. He tore off half and handed it across the furnace aisle.

"Eat," he said gruffly. "The storm won't wait for your stomach to catch up."

Lyra took the bread with blackened fingers, tearing into the warm, smoky crust. The salt and fat cut through the persistent alkaline tang of the aether clouds outside, grounding her in the heavy reality of wood and iron.

"Combine patrol skiff sighted off the northern reef, Captain," she murmured between bites, gesturing toward the brass periscope tube beside the throttle quadrant. "Two miles back, riding the high thermal. They’re running without lanterns."

Orlov peered through the eyepiece, adjusting the knurled focus ring. Through the mist, two slender masts cut the cloud tops like shark fins, dark and predatory. A cold knot tightened in his belly, older than any gale.

"They won't follow us into the thermal chute," Orlov said, his voice flat as lead. He laid a scarred palm upon the bypass lever. "Seal the deck hatches. We're diving under the basalt arch."
